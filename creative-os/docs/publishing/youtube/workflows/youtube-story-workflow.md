# YouTube Story Workflow

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Draft
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-20
**Owner:** Tree Wishes Publishing

---

## 1. Classify and Verify

Start with an approved manuscript for original fiction or a source-backed research entry for Québec Ghost Stories and Québec Dark Folklore. Record the category, location, source URL or publication details, and the difference between documented material, folklore, reported paranormal claims, and creative interpretation.

Original fiction must be identified as fiction. Research-based stories must not invent evidence, locations, witnesses, photographs, or historical facts.

## 2. Choose a Format

Use the Short template for a 45-90 second single-moment narrative with one emotional turn. Use the long-form template for a fully narrated story, contextual research, or an expanded atmosphere-led adaptation. One subject may have both packages, but each needs its own review.

## 3. Prepare Narration

Write for a calm, intimate telling voice. Open with a concrete image or question, use short sentences around the central unsettling moment, and end with a thoughtful invitation rather than a cheap reveal. Time the read aloud; do not force a Short beyond its intended duration.

## 4. Prepare Visuals and Sound

Use dark cinematic Québec atmosphere: warm amber and sepia light, black shadows, subtle vintage texture, elegant antique typography, and restrained motion. Keep the image subject legible. Slow push-ins, gentle parallax, candle or lamp flicker, and restrained grain are preferred to rapid cuts.

Credit photography, artwork, archival imagery, and licensed media. Label AI-generated artwork as an artistic interpretation where relevant; never represent it as historical documentation. Use cleared music and ambience. Keep music under narration and avoid excessive stingers, gore, or jump scares.

## 5. Build Discovery Metadata

Draft several truthful title options. The description should state the category, source or fiction framing, artwork credit when needed, a natural website destination, and concise relevant hashtags. The thumbnail should feature one clear visual anchor and minimal readable text.

## 6. Review Before Publication

Confirm all of the following:

- The narration matches its manuscript or cited source.
- Fiction, legend, report, and fact are framed accurately.
- Location names, dates, and people are spelled consistently with the source.
- Visual, music, and archival credits are complete.
- The website link is correct and supports the stated destination.
- The thumbnail, title, audio, captions, and on-screen text work as one restrained Campfire Horror Tales experience.
- A human has approved the final rendered video and platform metadata.

## 7. Publish and Archive

Publish manually after approval. Save the final title, description, public URL, publication date, and useful performance observations in the draft. These records may inform later automation, but automation must remain subject to editorial approval.
