# French Translation Style Guide

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Draft
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-20
**Owner:** Tree Wishes Publishing

---

## Purpose

French translations are literary publications of the English canonical manuscript. The objective is a natural, atmospheric French reading experience, not word-for-word substitution.

## Principles

- Preserve Andrea's voice, atmosphere, suspense, ambiguity, pacing, characterization, plot, and meaning.
- Let French syntax read naturally; do not imitate English phrasing when a better literary French construction exists.
- Do not soften horror unnecessarily or add information absent from the English manuscript.
- Keep character names and Saint-Clair-des-Bois unchanged.
- Use appropriate official French forms for Québec place names where applicable.
- Make dialogue sound natural in Québec French while preserving each speaker's character and narrative tone.
- Flag uncertain cultural, linguistic, register, or historical choices for human review rather than guessing.

## Review Standard

Andrea reviews each translation for intent and voice. A fluent or native French literary reader should review it before the translation is marked approved or published.

## Source and Status

The English manuscript remains authoritative. Record translation status and reviewer details in the story's shared metadata or a translation-specific note; do not duplicate the full metadata solely because a French manuscript exists.
