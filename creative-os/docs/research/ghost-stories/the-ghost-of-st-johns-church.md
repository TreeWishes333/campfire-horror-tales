# The Ghost of St. John's Church
## Québec Ghost Stories

**Location:** Gore, Laurentians, Québec

**Category:** Ghost Story

---

# Overview

Hidden among the forests of Gore in Québec's Laurentians once stood a small country church that became one of the province's most enduring paranormal legends.

Built in **1858**, St. John's Anglican Church quietly served its rural community for generations. Over time, stories of ghostly encounters, unexplained sounds, and mysterious apparitions began to circulate. Paranormal investigators, ghost hunters, and curious visitors travelled to the isolated church hoping to witness something they couldn't explain.

Today, although the church no longer stands, its legend continues to live on.

---

# History

St. John's Anglican Church was constructed in 1858 to serve the small community of Shrewsbury in Gore Township.

As the surrounding community declined, the church became increasingly isolated. During the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, rumours spread online claiming the church was haunted. These stories attracted paranormal investigators, ghost hunters, and curious visitors from across Québec.

Unfortunately, the growing attention also brought vandalism to the historic site.

The church was eventually deconsecrated and was destroyed by fire in 2014. The cemetery remains as a reminder of the area's history.

---

# The Ghost Story

Visitors have reported unusual experiences around the church and cemetery for many years.

Stories describe:

- Strange sounds inside the empty church
- Feelings of being watched
- Apparitions among the gravestones
- Unexplained lights
- Paranormal investigations and séances

While many of these accounts remain anecdotal, they helped transform this quiet rural church into one of Québec's best-known haunted locations.

---

# Fact vs. Legend

One of the most persistent internet rumours claimed that the church was the site of a mass suicide.

There is **no credible historical evidence** supporting this claim.

Most historians believe the rumour spread online and was repeated over many years, becoming part of the church's paranormal reputation.

In many ways, the legend itself became more famous than the history.

Readers are invited to decide for themselves where history ends and folklore begins.
