## Instagram Post

### The Enchanted Forest

Fort Témiscamingue, Abitibi-Témiscamingue, Quebec

Deep within the forests of Abitibi-Témiscamingue lies an ancient grove where the trees grow in strange and twisted shapes.

The white cedars are bent, curved, and almost tormented in appearance. Some are more than a thousand years old.

Science says the unusual shapes can be explained by harsh climates, ice storms, and poor soil.

But folklore points to the nearby cemeteries, where some say the souls of the departed have entered the trees, leaving behind a tormented symbol of difficult lives.

And then there is the story of a little girl with a flute.

They say she sometimes appears among the trees, her music making the ancient cedars dance.

Perhaps the trees are simply shaped by nature.

Or perhaps they are dancing to the music of the little girl.

Some say they hear the moans of tormented souls.

Others hear the sound of a young girl's music.

I wonder what you would hear.

The Dark Folklore Series celebrates the legends and stories that have been passed down through Québec.

## Editorial Notes

- This post presents the story as Québec folklore, not historical fact.
- Science offers natural explanations for the unusual shapes of the trees.
- The folklore source describes spirits associated with the nearby Indigenous and Catholic cemeteries and the difficult lives represented by the twisted trees.
- The little girl with the flute is presented as a legend associated with the forest.
- Avoid broadly attributing the entire legend to Indigenous tradition unless a specific Indigenous source is identified.
- Keep the tone atmospheric, respectful, and curious rather than presenting the supernatural elements as proven.
