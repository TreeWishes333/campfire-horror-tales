# Campfire Horror Tales

## Story 002

# The Boy in the Bog

**Status:** Draft 02  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Notes:** Second developmental draft with improved pacing, atmosphere, and ending.

---

There was once a boy named Frankie.

Everyone in Saint-Clair-des-Bois knew him.

And everyone feared him.

Frankie loved making people afraid.

He picked on the younger children in the village square, beat up his little brother Paul whenever no one was looking, and had a cruel streak that seemed to grow darker every summer.

But it wasn't the children he enjoyed hurting most.

It was the animals.

He threw rocks at squirrels just to watch them tumble from the trees.

He caught fish only to cut them apart with rusty scissors.

Not because he was hungry.

Not because he needed food.

Simply because he liked watching them suffer.

But his favourite victims...

Were the frogs.

Every summer afternoon, children wandered down to Blackwater Bog, just beyond the edge of Les Pins Noirs, carrying little pails in hopes of catching great green bullfrogs.

The old-timers always warned children never to wander into the marsh alone.

Most laughed off the stories.

Children always do.

The marsh echoed with laughter.

Tiny hands carefully scooped through the reeds.

Every frog was treated like treasure.

Until Frankie arrived.

If there weren't any parents nearby...

The joy disappeared.

Frankie would snatch a child's pail before anyone could stop him.

He'd hold the frightened frog high above his head while the children begged him to let it go.

He never did.

Instead, he smiled.

Slowly...

Deliberately...

He tore off its legs.

The frog kicked helplessly in his hand.

Then Frankie squeezed until its tiny body burst between his fingers.

Laughing, he flung what remained toward the horrified children.

Some screamed.

Some cried.

One little boy threw up.

Frankie laughed harder.

Because the frog had never been the point.

The transition from joy...

To terror...

That was Frankie's favourite part.

Before long, the children stopped catching frogs altogether.

Why bother?

Frankie always found them.

When there were no frogs left in the children's pails, Frankie started hunting through the bog himself.

Sometimes he found one.

Sometimes he didn't.

But the children...

And the frogs...

Had finally had enough.

The older children began digging.

The younger ones carried pails of mud away from the growing hole.

For days.

Then weeks.

Nearly the entire summer.

They dug deep into the soft marsh along the edge of the Bog, where the reeds met the towering pines.

When the hole was finally finished, they covered it with weeds, moss and reeds until it disappeared beneath the marsh.

Sometimes their own boots sank ankle-deep while they worked.

Perfect.

Their plan was simple.

Frankie would sink to his waist.

Then they would throw mud at him.

Laugh.

Mock him.

Embarrass him.

Maybe then he'd understand what it felt like to be helpless.

The children had planned a lesson.

The frogs...

Had planned a punishment.

Every evening after the children returned home, the frogs gathered.

Hundreds.

Then thousands.

They churned the black mud with their powerful little legs until the marsh became softer than anyone imagined possible.

Not waist deep.

Neck deep.

The water grew unnaturally still.

The bog seemed to awaken.

The trap was ready.

The next morning arrived warm and bright.

Just as the children had hoped...

Frankie came.

He shoved his way past them and stomped straight into the reeds looking for frogs.

His first step sank slightly.

His second sank deeper.

By the third...

The bog swallowed him.

His knees disappeared.

Then his waist.

His blue eyes grew wide with panic.

"What the hell?"

He twisted violently.

The more he fought...

The deeper he sank.

"Help me, you idiots!"

he screamed.

The children stood silently along the edge of the marsh.

A few giggled nervously.

None of them moved.

Then...

The still waters rippled.

One frog appeared.

Then another.

Then dozens.

Then hundreds.

Soon the entire bog seemed alive.

Thousands of frogs surrounded Frankie, watching him without a sound.

For the first time in his life...

Frankie was the frightened one.

He opened his mouth to scream.

That...

Was his final mistake.

The frogs leapt.

One after another they disappeared into his open mouth.

He gagged.

Coughed.

Thrashed wildly.

More came.

Then more.

They wriggled down his throat until his screams became nothing more than choking gurgles.

The children stood frozen.

No one laughed anymore.

No one moved.

No one dared help him.

By the time the bog grew still once again...

Frankie was gone.

Some say the marsh swallowed him whole.

Others say the frogs hollowed him out from the inside.

No one really knows.

But even today...

Children who visit that old bog never harm the frogs.

Some even leave one small flower beside the reeds before they head home.

Not because they're afraid of the bog.

Because they know...

The frogs are still watching.

Waiting patiently...

For the next Frankie.
