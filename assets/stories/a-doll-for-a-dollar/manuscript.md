# A Doll for a Dollar

Every summer, Saint-Clair-des-Bois held its annual village fair.

It was one of those weekends when the whole town seemed to spill into the streets. There were tables everywhere, old tools, antiques, children's toys, homemade preserves, baked goods, clothes, books, and things people had pulled out of barns and garages and decided they didn't need anymore.

I liked wandering through those kinds of sales.

You never really knew what you were going to find.

I wasn't looking for anything in particular that day.

That's when I noticed the little boy.

He was probably ten or eleven years old and sitting behind a small folding table by himself.

He looked a little disheveled, like he'd been outside all morning. His hair needed cutting, his clothes were rumpled, and there was dirt on his knees.

On the table was a strange collection of things.

A few little objects I couldn't identify.

Some odds and ends.

And several homemade tin-can telephones.

You know the kind.

Two tin cans with a string stretched between them.

One can for talking.

One for listening.

I remember smiling when I saw them.

There was something sweet about the idea of a kid making them himself and trying to sell them at the village fair.

Beside the telephones was a doll.

I stopped.

She was an old Raggedy Ann doll, and she called my attention immediately.

I had one when I was a little girl, I picked her up and looked her over.

She was old. So familiar, not necessarily valuable. Just special.

She was in great shape, but also looked like a doll that had belonged to someone before. I would called her well loved, but she might have sat on a shelf at least.

I held her tightly in my hands and looked back at the little boy.

"How much?"

He looked at the doll.

Then at me. He smiled, and pointed to his sign.

"One dollar."

There was a handwritten $1 sign sitting on the table beside the tin-can telephones.

I reached into my wallet. Of course, I didn't have a dollar.

I had a five-dollar bill.

I handed it to him.

His whole face changed. He looked genuinely excited.

He quickly pulled a little plastic container toward him and dumped the coins onto the table.

There couldn't have been much.

A handful of change.

He counted it carefully, seemed to be nickles, dimes, quarters, and even pennies.

he counted small change until it made a dollar, pushed it toward me and said. "
One.", continued conting to "Two", then "Three" and finally "Four" dollars.

He pushed the small mountain of coins toward me.

"There."

I laughed.

"Are you sure?"

He nodded.

I picked up the changed and dropped it into my pocket, thanked him, and I walked away with the doll.

It wasn't until I had gone around the corner that I started thinking about what had just happened.

I looked down at the doll and thought, why didn't i just tell him to keep the change?

I'd taken all his change.

It suddenly seemed wrong.

The kid had probably spent the whole morning sitting there, hoping to make a few dollars at the fair.

And I'd just taken all of it.

I turned around.

I decided I'd find him and give him the money back.

But when I went back around the corner, he was gone.

So was the table.

I walked around for a while looking for him.

There were people everywhere.

Tables.

Cars.

Children.

Music.

The smell of food.

But I couldn't find the little boy.

Eventually, I gave up.
I felt guilty though, and told my family when i got home. They all agreed it was pretty rotton of me.

I thought about him for days afterward.

Thouhgt of him counting the change, and also.....the way he'd looked when I handed him the five-dollar bill.

He hadn't looked like a kid who had just made a dollar.

He looked like a kid who was....relieved?

Years later, I still think about that, and how he had actually been excited about selling the doll, more than about making a dollar.

Someone had finally taken it.

Thats how I got the doll, so then what?

Well, the doll came home with me.

At first, I loved her.

She reminded me of being a kid.

I put her on a shelf.

She stayed there for a while.

A few people told me she was creepy....
i put her with the twilght zone movies I had on VHS, next to another doll bought at the yard sale (creepy nun doll), but one day, I decided she didn't really fit there, and I brought her home to the city.

but no matter where I placed her, I would find her on the ground, so I decided to put her away in the basement, in a box with the rest of the forgotten toys.

the next day, I found her sitting back on a shelf in my house.

I assumed someone had brought her back up. I asked around, but no one had.

I laughed it off.

Someone must be playing with me i thought

That was the only explanation.

So I put her away again.

This time, I put her in a box and taped it shut
the next morning she was on my dresser! Smiling at me!

I decided to take her back to the cottage, as she seemed at least to stay put on the shelf up there, crazy as it sounds I felt she wanted to go back, that she wasn't happy in the city, and definately not in a box!
and so she was returned to the twon she knew so long, the fresh mountain air.....

and she stayed put there on that shelf.

she seemed to fit there in the country, like old furniture, antique tools, VHS tapes, stained and tattered playing cards....

Objects that have spent generations in barns and old houses.

The doll definatly seemed to belong there.

but the boy still haunted me....I couldn't explain it, but it was like he and the doll were also connected.

Still, every summer, when I come back to Saint-Clair-des-Bois fair, I think about the little boy. Maybe not so little anymore.

I still look for him.

I don't know why.

Maybe I still want to give him the money. Maybe I wanted to tell him that I hadn't meant to take all of his change.

Maybe I want to ask him about the doll.

Mostly, I want to know what had happened to him.

But I.ve never seen him again.

Not once.

until tonight......well, I didn't see him thats true......but somethignstrange occured.

The tin-can telephone.

The room was dark except for the light coming through the glass.

I noticed something on the floor, near the book shelf where the doll sits, and at first, I thought it was a piece of string.

I bent down to pull it out from under the shelf, thats when I heard the tinkle of the cans......they were attached to the string....

I stared in disbelief for a moment, and then, I don't know why I did it, but I lifted one of the cans to my mouth.

"Hello kid, where did you go" I whispered carefully, voice shaking a little with fear...

Nothing happened.

Then I put the other can to my ear.

For a moment, there was only silence.

Then I heard something.

A faint sound.

Like someone breathing.

I froze.

I pulled the can away from my ear.

I looked around the room, and then to the doll sitting on the shelf.....our eyes locked as I slowly put the can back to my ear....

And then I heard his voice, very quietly, very close.

"why nowhere..... I am right here where I belong, where I have always belonged.....in a cabin in these woods"

I dropped the can.

I never brought the doll back to the city, never tried to give it away or put it back in a box......because some things are better left where they belong, and besides they will always find their way home.
